SOC LAB: SUSPICIOUS LOGIN INVESTIGATION

DATA AND SCOPE
15 synthetic authentication records.
No real accounts were accessed or changed.
All follow-up scenarios are fictional.
No containment actions were performed.

DETECTION RULE
Five or more failed logins followed by a successful login
for the same account and source IP within five minutes.

RESULTS
Two alerts generated: charlie and diana.
An alert identifies a suspicious sequence, not proof of compromise.


CASE 01: CHARLIE

CSV EVIDENCE
Source IP: 198.51.100.50
Failed attempts: 5
First failure: 2026-10-05 10:00:00 UTC
Successful login: 2026-10-05 10:02:30 UTC
Elapsed time: 2 minutes 30 seconds

INITIAL ASSESSMENT
The sequence meets the detection threshold.
Authentication records alone cannot establish whether the
successful login was authorised.

HYPOTHETICAL FOLLOW-UP
For this training scenario, assume:
- Charlie denies making the successful login.
- The device is unfamiliar.

These are fictional assumptions, not findings from the CSV.

SCENARIO ASSESSMENT
Suspected account compromise requiring escalation.
The assumed user denial and unfamiliar device increase concern,
but further evidence is needed to determine the incident scope.

PROPOSED RESPONSE
1. Preserve authentication and related activity logs.
2. Review MFA results, device details and post-login activity.
3. Escalate to the senior SOC analyst.
4. Follow the approved response playbook and authorisation
   process for session revocation and password reset.
5. Consider temporary account disablement based on the
   investigation findings and approved procedure.
6. Document decisions and monitor for further suspicious activity.

DISPOSITION
Escalation proposed under the fictional scenario.
Compromise is not confirmed by the supplied CSV.


CASE 02: DIANA

CSV EVIDENCE
Source IP: 203.0.113.25
Failed attempts: 5
First failure: 2026-10-05 11:00:00 UTC
Successful login: 2026-10-05 11:02:30 UTC
Elapsed time: 2 minutes 30 seconds

INITIAL ASSESSMENT
The sequence meets the same detection threshold as Charlie's.
The CSV alone does not establish a benign or malicious cause.

HYPOTHETICAL FOLLOW-UP
For this training scenario, assume:
- Diana confirms repeated password typing errors.
- The device matches her usual device.
- MFA completed successfully.
- A review identifies no unusual post-login activity.

These are fictional assumptions, not findings from the CSV.

SCENARIO ASSESSMENT
Likely benign authentication activity.
The combined scenario context supports this assessment;
successful MFA alone would not establish that a login is benign.

PROPOSED RESPONSE
1. Document the assumed user confirmation and supporting context.
2. Record why the sequence met the detection threshold.
3. Close as benign under the team's review procedure.
4. Do not propose containment based on this scenario.

DISPOSITION
Benign closure proposed under the fictional scenario.
The rule correctly detected its target pattern, but the
scenario does not indicate malicious activity.


LIMITATIONS
- Synthetic data provides limited authentication context.
- No actual MFA, device or post-login records were supplied.
- User confirmation was assumed, not obtained.
- Neither account's real-world status can be determined.
- All response actions are proposals only.

KEY LESSON
Identical alert patterns can have different explanations.
Separate observed evidence from assumptions, seek additional
context and document the reasoning behind each decision.
